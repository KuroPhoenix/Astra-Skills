# Astra Report book distillation — reporting structure and review workflow

**Date:** 2026-08-18

**Revised:** 2026-08-19 — preserved the books' unified-author advice and separated its
integration into producer-owned case, Report-owned route, user-owned outcome, and
producer-recorded lifecycle effect

**Status:** research and design-exploration record only; this document changes no Astra
design, lifecycle authority, implementation plan, runtime, roster, or ledger

**Question:** What do the seven locally supplied books actually suggest about presenting
many technical topics without overwhelming the reader, and how do those findings bear on a
reviewable Report artifact?

---

## 1. Scope, method, and evidence labels

This is a **comprehensive-enough distillation of the chapters relevant to Astra Report**, not
a cover-to-cover summary of every book. Chapters about unrelated subjects—for example,
graphic selection, games, collaborative instruction, or proofreading mechanics—were not
summarized merely to increase the rule count. Relevant chapters were inspected directly in
the local books, including chapters outside the existing 15-rule method canon.

Only the seven local books listed below were used as evidence. No reviews, blog summaries,
or other secondary accounts were used. Findings are paraphrased; no long passages are
reproduced.

Every proposed design implication is marked:

- **[D] Direct source support:** the source addresses the same or a closely matching
  communication operation, such as organizing a multi-topic document or requesting action.
- **[A] Analogical transfer:** the source studies a different activity—usually learning from
  multimedia or navigating instructional material—and the possible reporting application
  remains a hypothesis.
- **[S] Astra synthesis:** the implication combines source ideas with Astra's artifact and
  authority model. The books do not establish the mechanism.

These labels describe evidential distance, not authority. Even a directly supported writing
practice cannot change producer-owned facts, severity, consequences, decisions, approval,
or workflow state.

### 1.1 Authority-transfer method

The seven books generally assume that **the writer or instructional designer owns both the
case being made and the route by which the reader encounters it**. Astra Report does not.
This distillation therefore preserves two layers instead of assigning each book's whole
writer role to Report:

- **Original advice:** what the book tells its writer or designer to decide or do. This is
  retained even when Astra assigns part of that role elsewhere.
- **Astra integration:** the same advice after applying the governing split: **the producer
  owns the case; Report owns the route through it; the user owns the outcome**.

For this transfer, **case** includes every substantive claim, purpose, relevance judgment,
consequence, severity, required action, decision option, preferred choice, content-essentialness
judgment, source relationship, and lifecycle disposition. The producer must supply these
semantics through an authoritative artifact or producer-owned packet. **Route** includes
faithful wording, ordering, grouping already-related material, headings, pacing, section
markers, navigation, and source links. Report may use those operations to guide the user
toward comprehension or decision-readiness; it may not use them to manufacture a conclusion
or preferred choice. **Outcome** includes the user's review, comment, choice, approval, or
rejection. Report may record the user's exact response, but only the producer may interpret
and apply it within its lifecycle authority.

Each implication below therefore states both the book's original advice and the permitted
Astra integration. If a required case input is absent, the valid transfer is to name the gap
or present exact source material—not to infer the missing intent, significance, consequence,
action, or recommendation.

**Subsequent user-approved integration clarification (2026-08-19).** The authority split above
does not require a neutral or stance-free Report. When presenting a producer case, Report is a
delegated author and guide for that producer's recorded position; when confirming and returning
feedback, it represents the user's position; and when stating revelation, review, or delivery
facts, it speaks only from its own procedural jurisdiction. These principals alternate in
separately labelled blocks inside one bounded, stance-bearing Report Artifact. Typed feedback is
draft material until Report offers a polished rendering and the user confirms one final wording;
only that confirmed wording becomes the durable user-position event. This paragraph records an
Astra design decision, not advice established by the seven books.

## 2. Bottom line

The collection does **not** yield one canonical Report model. In particular, it does not
select Capsule/NOW/NEXT/menu, fixed numeric surface budgets, an Exposure Ledger, or any
other Astra-specific protocol. Nor does it directly describe a non-authoritative briefing
layer: most of its advice assumes that the person shaping the communication also owns its
purpose, central claim, relevance, consequence, and recommendation.

Its value to Astra is therefore dual-layered:

1. **Original advice:** let the communication's job and reader question determine its shape;
   separate distinct topics; group related support; make the point, relevance, consequence,
   and requested response findable; explain unfamiliar terms plainly; preserve necessary
   qualification; and use progressive access only when its navigation cost is justified.
2. **Astra integration:** the producer supplies the job, substantive point, relevance,
   consequence, action, recommendation, essential content, and source relationships. Report
   may faithfully word, order, pace, mark, and link that supplied case. The user alone reviews
   it and chooses an outcome; the producer alone gives that outcome lifecycle effect.
3. **Review workflow:** Redish directly supports partial-draft review, section-specific
   comments, deliberate comment disposition, and a record of changes made and not made. An
   internal Report artifact may analogically record revelation and exact user responses, but
   its records do not establish comprehension, approval, or producer disposition.

The user's repeated block—`TOPIC → MOTIVATION → WHAT IS IT? → IMPACT → ACTION`—is a
well-supported **candidate route for a producer-supplied multi-topic case**, not a universal
grammar and not permission for Report to fill an absent field with its own judgment.

## 3. Book-by-book distillation

## 3.1 Barbara Minto — *The Minto Pyramid Principle*

### Reporting-relevant thesis

A reader should encounter a governing point before being asked to reconstruct it from
details. Supporting points must answer the question raised by the point above them and must
be grouped and ordered by a defensible logic. Crucially, Minto does not prescribe one
universal document template: the reader's question and the communication's purpose
determine whether the document directs action, requests approval, explains a method,
compares alternatives, or reviews progress.

### Key mechanisms and locators

| Mechanism | Distilled finding | Precise locator |
|---|---|---|
| Governing idea first | Put the summarizing idea above the detail that supports it; do not make the reader derive it unaided. | Part I, ch. 1, printed pp. 1–11 |
| Vertical question/answer relation | Each lower level answers the question created by the statement above it; horizontal groups require a logical relation. | Part I, ch. 2, pp. 12–20 |
| Reader-question-driven openings | Establish the situation needed to recognize a problem or question, then provide the answer; different document jobs raise different questions. | Part I, ch. 4, pp. 34–59 |
| Explicit ordering | Use time/causal sequence, structural decomposition, or degree/importance rather than arbitrary list order. | Part II, ch. 6, pp. 75–93 |
| Meaningful synthesis | Category labels alone are not summaries; state what the grouped points collectively mean or what effect the actions produce. | Part II, ch. 7, pp. 94–118 |
| Navigable long documents | Headings, indented structure, and concise progress-review pages can support discussion one portion at a time. Summaries are warranted by length or complexity, not ritual. | Part IV, ch. 10, pp. 170–188, especially pp. 180–181 |
| Disciplined next steps | A next step belongs at the end only when it follows uncontroversially; a disputed action belongs in the reasoning body. | Part IV, ch. 10, pp. 184–188 |

### Implications for multi-topic technical reports

- **Original advice [D]:** Give each topic a governing claim, then group the explanation,
  consequence, and evidence that answer the questions raised by it. **Astra integration
  [S]:** the producer supplies the governing claim, consequence, and support relationships;
  Report may place the claim first and arrange its linked support without synthesizing a new
  conclusion.
- **Original advice [D]:** Choose the document shape from the job being done; status,
  approval, comparison, explanation, and review need not have identical field order.
  **Astra integration [S]:** the producer declares the communicative job and requested
  response; Report selects a faithful route and field order for that job.
- **Original advice [D]:** Let a heading expose the topic's actual point rather than a broad
  category whose significance the reader must infer. **Astra integration [S]:** the producer
  supplies that point; Report may turn it into a concise heading linked to the source ID. If
  no point is supplied, Report uses a neutral label and names the missing case input.
- **Original advice [D]:** Order groups by a defensible logic such as sequence, structure, or
  importance. **Astra integration [S]:** Report may apply those ordering mechanics only to
  producer-supplied dependencies, consequence ranks, or reader questions. The user still
  owns the response to the ordered case.

### Cautions and limits

Minto's business-document setting is close to reporting, but its writer normally owns the
answer and argument as well as their presentation. It does not establish an AI interaction
protocol, and its discussion of how many items a person can hold should not be used as modern
evidence for an exact surface budget. “Answer first” licenses Report to foreground a
producer-supplied answer, never to invent one.

## 3.2 Todd Rogers and Jessica Lasky-Fink — *Writing for Busy Readers*

### Reporting-relevant thesis

The scarce resource is reader attention and response effort. Effective communication makes
the goal and priority easy to find, reduces competing ideas and requests, organizes related
material together, explains why the reader should care, and lowers the work required to take
the intended action. “Less” concerns ideas, requests, and friction as well as words.

### Key mechanisms and locators

| Mechanism | Distilled finding | Precise locator |
|---|---|---|
| Attention-aware writing | Readers selectively attend, skim, scan, and switch tasks; communication should provide a clear anchor. | Chs. 1–2, local PDF pp. 21–44 |
| Goal before drafting | Know what the communication should enable the reader to understand or do. | Ch. 3, local PDF pp. 45–48 |
| Fewer competing demands | Remove secondary ideas and requests that obscure the main goal; short wording alone is insufficient. | Ch. 4, local PDF pp. 49–66 |
| Familiar expression | Prefer words and sentence structures the intended reader can process readily. | Ch. 5, local PDF pp. 67–85 |
| Navigation and grouping | Make purpose and priority visible, separate distinct topics, group related material, and order intentionally. | Ch. 6, local PDF pp. 86–109 |
| Selective formatting | Formatting can direct attention, but too many signals compete and can reverse the benefit. | Ch. 7, local PDF pp. 110–122 |
| Reader relevance | Explain why the information matters to the reader rather than assuming its significance is obvious. | Ch. 8, local PDF pp. 123–132 |
| Response effort | Put action information together, narrow the request, and reduce the number of steps required to respond. | Ch. 9, local PDF pp. 133–147 |
| Long and multi-part communications | Bundle pieces that support the same event/action and require similar effort; split unrelated pieces or pieces requiring different effort. Use signposting and move necessary lower-priority material to an attachment or equivalent layer. | Ch. 10, local PDF pp. 148–164, especially pp. 150–153 |

### Implications for multi-topic technical reports

- **Original advice [D]:** Count independent ideas and requests, not only words; five short,
  unrelated asks can be harder than one coherent explanation. **Astra integration [S]:** the
  producer identifies claims and requests; Report may budget and stage those supplied
  surfaces without deleting or merging their meaning.
- **Original advice [D]:** State why a topic deserves attention and put the complete bounded
  ask in one place. **Astra integration [S]:** `MOTIVATION` and `ACTION` must come from
  producer-supplied relevance and action fields. Report may make them findable and plainly
  worded, but it may not decide why the user should care or what the user should do.
- **Original advice [D]:** Split matters requiring different decisions or effort, and group
  support for the same decision. **Astra integration [S]:** the producer supplies decision
  identity and topic/evidence relationships; Report uses those relationships for grouping and
  pacing rather than deriving them from prose.
- **Original advice [D]:** Preview the pieces and their significance in a long communication.
  **Astra integration [S]:** Report writes concise previews of producer-supplied conclusions,
  relevance, and caveats and retains their source links; it does not create significance to
  improve information scent.
- **Original advice [D]:** Reduce competing demands. **Astra integration [S]:** reply-surface
  budgeting is a plausible Report-owned routing mechanism, but the book supplies neither a
  numeric limit nor a formal reply-surface construct. The user remains free to inspect all
  supplied surfaces and decide the outcome.

### Cautions and limits

The book repeatedly makes context matter, but its writer is also expected to define the
communication goal, reader relevance, and desired response. Astra must obtain those from the
producer. The book does not support deleting qualifications, forcing every communication
below an arbitrary word count, or splitting every multi-part message. Shared technical terms
can be efficient; unexplained unfamiliar terms transfer work back to the reader.

## 3.3 Janice (Ginny) Redish — *Letting Go of the Words*

### Reporting-relevant thesis

People approach information with goals and questions rather than a desire to read a small
book. Content should be organized into meaningful topics, expose its key message and route,
use words the intended reader understands, and let deeper material remain reachable without
destroying the reader's sense of place. Drafting and review are also explicit workflows, not
an invisible final-stage event.

### Key mechanisms and locators

| Mechanism | Distilled finding | Precise locator |
|---|---|---|
| “Topic, not book” | Break content by task, question, sequence, audience, or information type; keep together what belongs to the reader's scenario. | Ch. 5, pp. 69–92 |
| Key message and layering | Readers scan; put the essential point early, chunk dense material, and layer short orientation with detail while preserving location. | Ch. 6, pp. 93–125, especially pp. 102–106 and 114–121 |
| Plain, explicit prose | Visible actors, active verbs, common words, and given-before-new flow improve access even for serious technical material. | Ch. 8, pp. 171–204 |
| Headings as an outline | Headings provide context and a scan path. Question, statement, and action headings serve different jobs; generic noun labels can fail when readers do not share the taxonomy. | Ch. 10, pp. 235–262 |
| Descriptive destinations | Links should identify what the reader will get; vague “more” links have poor information scent. Similar destinations need coordinated descriptions. | Ch. 12, pp. 307–328 |
| Partial-draft review | Share partial drafts early to test level, organization, tone, and message before the whole document is fixed. | Ch. 13, pp. 329–344, especially pp. 335–337 |
| Review contract and disposition | Set reviewer expectations and scope, read every comment, do not automatically accept proposed changes, investigate conflicts, and keep a summary of changes made and not made for later rounds. | Ch. 13, pp. 339–343 |

### Implications for multi-topic technical reports

- **Original advice [D]:** Use headings as a useful outline, not decorative labels.
  **Astra integration [S]:** the producer supplies each topic's identity and substantive key
  message; Report may create the textual outline, faithful headings, position markers, and
  source links that route through them.
- **Original advice [D]:** Give optional detail a descriptive destination and keep essential
  action or qualification on the main path. **Astra integration [S]:** the producer identifies
  significance, required action, and essential qualifications; Report writes faithful
  previews and controls revelation. It may not classify producer content as optional merely
  to shorten the first view.
- **Original advice [D]:** Explain technical content in common language without losing the
  needed exact term. **Astra integration [S]:** Report owns that faithful wording operation;
  the underlying definition, fact, and qualification remain producer-owned.
- **Original advice [D]:** Review a tangible draft, anchor comments to it, and carry a
  made/not-made disposition summary across rounds. **Astra integration [S]:** the internal
  Report artifact may hold stable section anchors and exact user comments; the user owns the
  comment, while the producer alone decides and records its lifecycle disposition.
- **Original advice [D]:** Set reviewer expectations and examine every comment rather than
  accepting edits automatically. **Astra integration [A]:** Report may present the
  producer-supplied review scope and record responses, but cannot accept, reject, or implement
  a comment.
- **Original advice [D]:** Preserve the reader's place while layering detail. **Astra
  integration [S]:** using the same internal artifact as a local revelation record is an
  Astra option. Redish supports the workflow, not its data model, status vocabulary, or
  approval semantics.

### Cautions and limits

Redish writes about web content and assumes its writer can define key messages and dispose of
review comments; it does not describe Astra's separated lifecycle authority. Layering is not
permission to hide a producer-identified blocker. A topic label is not automatically
comprehensible; if the reader is swamped by terms, Report should pair the producer's exact
term with a faithful plain-language explanation. Comments are user inputs for the producer
to consider, not changes for Report to apply.

## 3.4 Joseph M. Williams and Joseph Bizup — *Style: Lessons in Clarity and Grace*

### Reporting-relevant thesis

Clarity is produced by relations the reader can follow: important actors perform actions,
sentences move from familiar material to new material, important points receive emphasis,
and sections establish the problem that makes their claim worth reading. Concision is not
terseness; calibrated qualifications can be part of the meaning.

### Key mechanisms and locators

| Mechanism | Distilled finding | Precise locator |
|---|---|---|
| Characters and actions | Put important actors in subjects and important actions in verbs. | Lesson 3, pp. 28–45 |
| Cohesion | Begin from material the reader can recognize, then introduce new or complex information. | Lesson 5, pp. 64–78, especially pp. 66–69 |
| Emphasis | Place material where readers naturally give it stress; move from simpler framing toward complexity. | Lesson 6, pp. 79–93 |
| Motivation | A useful introduction establishes shared context, a condition that creates a problem, the cost of leaving it unresolved, and a response. Practical problems call for action; conceptual ones call for changed understanding. | Lesson 7, pp. 94–108, especially pp. 96–103 |
| Global coherence | Start a section with a short segment that gives its point and themes. Templates help only when the underlying content supports them. | Lesson 8, pp. 109–121, especially pp. 110–118 |
| Responsible concision | Remove needless wording without stripping useful texture, precision, or hedges that communicate certainty. | Lesson 9, pp. 122–136, especially pp. 130–134 |
| Shape | Put the point near the beginning and frame complex support, while allowing context to precede a point when readers need it. | Lesson 10, pp. 137–159, especially pp. 139–144 |

### Implications for multi-topic technical reports

- **Original advice [D]:** Establish the condition, problem or cost, central claim, and
  practical response that make a section worth reading. **Astra integration [S]:** the
  producer supplies the content for `MOTIVATION`, `IMPACT`, and `ACTION`; Report may arrange
  and phrase it but cannot construct the problem, cost, solution, or main claim.
- **Original advice [D]:** Name responsible actors and actions plainly, then introduce exact
  vocabulary as needed. **Astra integration [S]:** Report may render `WHAT IS IT?` using those
  sentence mechanics while preserving producer-supplied facts and artifact identities.
- **Original advice [D]:** Preserve calibrated qualifications such as “observed,” “likely,”
  “untested,” and “proven.” **Astra integration [S]:** certainty is part of the producer's
  case; Report may simplify syntax but must preserve its calibration and source link.
- **Original advice [D]:** Use templates only when their content warrants them. **Astra
  integration [S]:** Report may repeat a stable field order for scanning, but an absent
  motivation, impact, recommendation, or action remains a named producer-contract gap. The
  user, not the template, determines the outcome.

### Cautions and limits

This is a prose and argumentation book whose writer constructs the problem and response; it
is not evidence that Report may do so or evidence for progressive disclosure. Point-first and
context-first are not absolute opposites: producer-supplied context may precede a point when
the user needs it to understand that point. The book explicitly treats templates as useful
but potentially rigid.

## 3.5 Mayer and Fiorella, eds. — *The Cambridge Handbook of Multimedia Learning*

### Reporting-relevant thesis

The Handbook concerns learning from multimedia, so most transfer to Astra Report is
analogical. Its strongest contribution is not a ready-made report format but disciplined
boundary conditions: processing capacity is limited, cues and segments can guide processing,
prior knowledge changes what guidance helps, free navigation can impose its own cognitive
and metacognitive work, and multiple-source comprehension requires retaining source–claim
relationships and disagreement.

### Key mechanisms and locators

| Mechanism | Distilled finding | Precise locator |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence discipline | Popular instructional claims can be confounded; compare content, function, method, and objective outcomes rather than preference alone. | Ch. 3, pp. 25–40 |
| Limited capacity and active processing | Learners select, organize, and integrate information under capacity limits; task complexity and prior knowledge affect load. | Chs. 5–6, pp. 57–81 |
| Expertise reversal | Detailed guidance that helps novices may cease to help or may hinder experienced learners. | Ch. 13, pp. 171–184 |
| Coherence and signaling | Excluding truly extraneous material and cueing organization can help under relevant conditions; cue effects vary with material and prior knowledge. | Ch. 14, pp. 185–198; ch. 17, pp. 221–230 |
| Segmenting and pre-training | Meaningful learner-paced segments and prior knowledge of central concepts can reduce essential overload in complex, fast instructional material. Optimal segment size and generality remain conditional. | Ch. 19, pp. 243–260, especially pp. 243–245 and 256–257 |
| Learner control | Control over pace, sequence, selection, and representation are different. Broad free control has limited evidence and works best with prior knowledge plus orientation/self-regulation support. | Ch. 35, pp. 418–429, especially pp. 418–421 and 426–427 |
| Multiple documents | Readers must link claims to sources, integrate supporting or conflicting accounts, and preserve “who says what”; making source information salient can help. | Ch. 44, pp. 521–536, especially pp. 521–525 and 532–534 |

### Implications for multi-topic technical reports

- **Original advice [A]:** Use signals to orient attention and meaningful segments to pace
  complex material. **Astra integration [S]:** Report may add a compact route marker such as
  `2/5 Testing`, control section revelation, and preserve a visible path. The marker records
  neither producer work state nor user understanding.
- **Original advice [A]:** Segment around meaningful units, while recognizing that free
  navigation creates its own work. **Astra integration [S]:** the producer supplies semantic
  topic boundaries and relationships; Report chooses pacing and navigation over those units
  rather than inventing a decomposition of the case.
- **Original advice [A]:** Establish central concepts before asking a learner to integrate
  them. **Astra integration [S]:** the producer identifies the necessary concepts and their
  relationships; Report may provide a short faithful gloss before presenting their linked
  detail.
- **Original advice [A]:** Preserve source–claim relations and disagreements across documents.
  **Astra integration [S]:** the producer supplies claim identities and source relationships;
  Report keeps the links visible and does not adjudicate a contradiction.
- **Original advice [D]:** Evaluate a design by objective performance under stated boundary
  conditions, not preference alone. **Astra integration [S]:** assess Report through user
  comprehension and decision performance against the producer-owned case, together with
  navigation cost and authority-preservation errors.
- **Original advice [A]:** Reduce avoidable load without impeding essential processing.
  **Astra integration [S]:** Report may remove its own presentational clutter; producer-owned
  essentialness, numeric surface limits, and any progressive-report state machine are not
  established by the Handbook.

### Cautions and limits

The dependent variable is generally learning, often from words plus pictures, not engineering
supervision through a text report. Its instructional designer normally chooses the objective,
central concepts, and essential material; Astra assigns those case judgments to the producer.
“Extraneous” cannot be redefined to include a producer-owned caveat merely because it is
inconvenient. Segmenting evidence does not imply tiny sections, and learner-control findings
warn against making the user perform all routing work.

## 3.6 Richard E. Mayer — *Multimedia Learning*

### Reporting-relevant thesis

Design should start from human sense-making and evidence rather than from the host
technology's capabilities. People do not merely receive information: they select, organize,
and integrate it. Design principles therefore need both a theory of how the reader processes
material and empirical tests of what works, for whom, under what conditions, and on what
outcome.

### Key mechanisms and locators

| Mechanism | Distilled finding | Precise locator |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence and boundary conditions | Principles should be theory-grounded, experimentally tested, and narrowed by learner, material, assessment, and medium. Much of the evidence base uses short laboratory tasks and immediate tests. | Preface, local PDF pp. 12–26 |
| Human-centered design | Contrast technology-centered design (“what can this medium do?”) with learner-centered design (“how can it aid cognition?”). | Ch. 1, local PDF pp. 31–78, especially pp. 45–54 |
| Construction, not delivery | Distinguish information acquisition from knowledge construction and distinguish retention from transfer/meaningful use. | Ch. 1, local PDF pp. 54–67 |
| Limited but active processing | Meaningful learning involves selecting, organizing, and integrating; processing demand can be extraneous, essential, or generative. | Ch. 2, local PDF pp. 79–143, especially pp. 80–82, 98–120, and 126–143 |

### Implications for multi-topic technical reports

- **Original advice [A]:** Begin with human sense-making goals rather than a medium's available
  controls. **Astra integration [S]:** the producer supplies the reporting task and intended
  substantive outcome; Report designs the route first and maps it to square icons, text, or
  other host affordances afterward.
- **Original advice [A]:** Judge success by usable understanding or transfer, not recall or
  layout preference alone. **Astra integration [S]:** evaluate whether the user understood and
  acted accurately on the producer-owned case; Report does not define the correct substantive
  answer.
- **Original advice [A]:** Manage processing demand so attention can support sense-making.
  **Astra integration [S]:** Report may remove renderer-authored distraction and pace supplied
  content while retaining every producer-designated fact, caveat, action, and evidence route.
- **Original advice [A]:** Help the reader select, organize, and integrate related material.
  **Astra integration [S]:** a Report section may connect producer-supplied topic, reason,
  consequence, and response through wording and layout. The relationships and response
  semantics cannot be invented to complete the pattern.

### Cautions and extraction limit

The supplied file is an **incomplete preview**. It contains the preface, chapter 1, and chapter
2, then ends at local PDF p. 151. It does not contain the book's chapters on coherence,
signaling, segmenting, pre-training, or the other named design principles. This document does
not attribute those absent chapters to L6; the complete Handbook and *e-Learning* file are
used for accessible versions of the relevant principles. Its learner-centered designer also
owns the learning objective and content; in Astra those substantive inputs remain with the
producer rather than Report.

## 3.7 Clark and Mayer — *e-Learning and the Science of Instruction*

### Reporting-relevant thesis

Instructional structure should be aligned to the goal, task, learner knowledge, and delivery
conditions. The book operationalizes coherence, segmentation, pre-training, conversational
language, and navigation, but it also shows that learner control and links can create load.
Because its subject is instruction, application to reporting remains analogical.

### Key mechanisms and locators

| Mechanism | Distilled finding | Precise locator |
|---|---|---|
| Goal and content type | Distinguish goals that inform from goals that support performance; analyze facts, concepts, processes, procedures, and strategic principles before choosing a method. | Ch. 1, pp. 7–28, especially pp. 13–18 |
| Evidence-aware design | Relate design claims to a model of learning and suitable research rather than media fashion. | Ch. 2, pp. 31–48 |
| Coherence | Remove audio, graphics, or words that are irrelevant to the learning goal; added interest or depth can interfere when it competes with essential processing. | Ch. 7, pp. 133–150 |
| Conversational expression | Direct, conversational language can improve some instructional outcomes, subject to audience and task. | Ch. 8, pp. 157–177 |
| Segmenting and pre-training | Break a complex continuous lesson into meaningful learner-paced parts and establish key concepts before a complex process. | Ch. 9, pp. 183–195 |
| Constrained control | Novices often make poor instructional selections; make important events the default, give pacing control, and consider guidance rather than unbounded choice. | Ch. 13, pp. 289–307 |
| Navigation | Use descriptive headings and introductory statements, use links sparingly for essential material, use maps mainly for orientation, and show position/progress. | Ch. 13, pp. 307–312 |

### Implications for multi-topic technical reports

- **Original advice [A]:** Identify the goal and content type before choosing the method.
  **Astra integration [S]:** the producer states whether a topic asks the user to understand,
  decide, inspect, or act and supplies the relevant content; Report emphasizes the fields and
  route serving that declared job.
- **Original advice [A]:** Segment complex content and establish unfamiliar concepts before
  their interactions. **Astra integration [S]:** the producer supplies topic boundaries,
  central concepts, and relationships; Report paces them and provides faithful plain-language
  orientation.
- **Original advice [A]:** Use descriptive headings, introductory statements, and position
  cues. **Astra integration [S]:** Report may render an outline and `2/5 Testing` marker from
  producer-supplied topic identities; the marker is navigation, not work or review state.
- **Original advice [A]:** Keep essential prerequisites on the main path and use links
  selectively. **Astra integration [S]:** the producer identifies essential decisions,
  qualifications, and supporting depth; Report controls placement and revelation but cannot
  demote required content.
- **Original advice [A]:** Prefer guided control when unbounded choice would burden the
  learner. **Astra integration [S]:** Report may recommend which producer-supplied section to
  inspect next using producer-supplied purpose, dependencies, and consequence fields, and may
  bound navigation. It may not turn a route recommendation into a preferred substantive
  option; the user owns that choice.

### Cautions and limits

This 2008 edition predates the 2022 Handbook's larger evidence base and studies e-learning,
not technical reporting. Its instructional designer owns goals, essential content, and
recommended learning paths; only the route portion transfers to Report. Its adaptive-control
discussion should not be imported as inferred attention, expertise, or recall state. The
relevant safe transfer is explicit user-selected depth or clear orientation, not hidden
psychological personalization.

## 4. Cross-book synthesis

### 4.1 Agreements

The books converge at the level of a conventional author who owns both case and route. The
right-hand clause below states the narrower transfer into Astra:

1. **Purpose precedes format.** Minto, Rogers and Lasky-Fink, Redish, Williams, and Clark and
   Mayer make the reader's question, goal, scenario, or task central. **Astra transfer:** the
   producer supplies that purpose; Report chooses the presentation route that serves it.
2. **Topic organization matters more than raw brevity.** Minto's logical groups, Redish's
   topics, and *Busy Readers*' idea/request economy converge here. **Astra transfer:** the
   producer supplies topic identities and substantive relationships; Report groups, orders,
   and stages them without changing the case.
3. **Relevance and consequence should be explicit.** Williams supplies problem/cost logic;
   *Busy Readers* asks why the reader should care; Minto asks the writer to state what grouped
   facts mean. **Astra transfer:** relevance, meaning, consequence, severity, and caveat are
   producer inputs. Report makes them findable and understandable.
4. **Action should be easy to locate and execute.** Minto distinguishes uncontroversial next
   steps from disputed recommendations; *Busy Readers* reduces response effort. **Astra
   transfer:** the producer supplies the action, options, consequences, and any preferred
   choice; Report positions them, and the user owns the decision.
5. **Orientation supports depth.** Headings, outlines, signals, and segments favor visible
   structure, with instructional evidence remaining analogical. **Astra transfer:** Report
   owns markers, pacing, navigation, and faithful previews over producer-defined content.
6. **Clarity is not “dumbing down.”** Redish and Williams make plain language compatible with
   technical precision and calibrated uncertainty. **Astra transfer:** Report may simplify
   wording, never the producer's semantic claim or degree of certainty.
7. **Source identity matters when claims differ.** The Handbook studies loss of source–claim
   links and disagreement. **Astra transfer:** the producer supplies provenance; Report keeps
   it visible, and neither Report nor the user interface manufactures consensus.

### 4.2 Tensions that a design decision must resolve

| Tension | Original advice | Astra authority constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Answer first vs. context first | Lead with the point when it is intelligible; otherwise supply the minimum needed condition first. | The producer supplies both point and necessary context. Report decides their faithful order, not their substance. |
| Stable template vs. task-specific structure | Repetition aids scanning, but structure remains conditional on purpose and content. | The producer declares purpose and topic semantics; Report chooses whether a stable route fits. |
| Progressive control vs. navigation work | Segmentation and pacing can help, while broad selection and link traversal add work. | Report controls revelation and navigation; it cannot defer producer-designated required content. |
| Brevity vs. qualification | Remove competing material but retain meaning-changing caveats and uncertainty. | Report removes its own clutter, not producer-owned qualifications, sources, or consequences. |
| Exact terminology vs. accessibility | Pair unfamiliar exact terms with understandable explanation. | Report may reword faithfully; the producer owns definitions and technical meaning. |
| Motivation before action vs. urgent action first | Informed review may need context first; urgency can favor action first. | A producer-supplied blocker or urgency flag may change Report's order. Report cannot declare urgency or recommend a substantive option. |

### 4.3 Concepts absent or not yet explicit in the current Astra Report design

This comparison uses `designs/astra-report.md` as inspected on 2026-08-18. “Absent” means
the concept is not yet an explicit contract, not that the repository has rejected it.

- **[S] Producer-declared report-job discriminator:** the producer identifies status,
  decision, explanation, audit, or review as the communicative job; Report chooses a route for
  it before applying a global layer model. The current design defines fixed Reader Brief
  layers but does not yet give these jobs distinct section grammars.
- **[S] Section-anchored review workflow:** use the internal report artifact as the review
  object and record exact comments at stable section IDs. Report can carry the comments; only
  the producer can carry a made/not-made lifecycle disposition into the next authoritative
  round. The current Exposure Ledger records presentation, not user comments or disposition.
- **[S] Separate state axes:** distinguish Report-owned delivery/revelation, explicit
  user-owned review/comment, user-owned outcome, and producer-owned lifecycle disposition.
  One code cannot safely represent all four.
- **[S] Plain-language relationship contract:** require a short familiar-language explanation
  of a producer-supplied technical term's recorded role and relationship when it is not
  already shared. Report may express, not create, that relationship.
- **[S] Calibrated-language check:** preserve source certainty and limitations at sentence
  level, not only as a general fidelity precedence.
- **[S] Navigation-cost check:** evaluate how many selections, returns, and taxonomy guesses
  the reader must make. Current staged delivery specifies what opens, but not a budget or
  measure for navigation effort.
- **[S] Reviewer-role contract:** identify what kind of review is requested and what the
  reviewer is authorized to comment on. The producer supplies that contract; Report presents
  it before treating an explicit user declaration of “reviewed” as meaningful.
- **[S] Authority-preservation check:** test whether Report introduced any unsupported
  purpose, relevance, conclusion, consequence, action, option, preference, or essentialness
  judgment while improving the route.

These are research findings for discussion. They are not amendments.

## 5. Evaluation of the repeated five-field topic block

The proposal is understood as **one repeated section for each report topic**, not as five
global sections for the whole report:

```text
[TOPIC]
  [MOTIVATION]
  [WHAT IS IT?]
  [IMPACT]
  [ACTION]
```

### 5.1 Field-by-field support

The sources support these fields as writing operations, but normally let the writer supply
their content. The last two columns perform the Astra authority transfer explicitly.

| Field | Original advice and evidence | Producer-owned case input | Report-owned allowed transformation | Failure to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `TOPIC` | **[D]** Minto's grouping, Redish's headings, and *Busy Readers*' separation of distinct matters. | Stable topic identity plus its label or governing point and substantive relationships. | Use a faithful short heading, order it among supplied topics, add a marker such as `2/5`, and link its source ID. | Inventing the point or relationship; treating position as completion, review, or approval. |
| `MOTIVATION` | **[D]** Williams' problem setup and *Busy Readers*' “why care” operation. | Recorded purpose, context, problem, or relevance judgment explaining why the topic is present. | Put that reason in plain language and at the point needed for comprehension, preserving qualification and provenance. | Inventing urgency, drama, project history, or reader relevance when the producer supplied none. |
| `WHAT IS IT?` | **[D + A]** Redish and Williams support plain explanation; pre-training research analogically supports establishing unfamiliar concepts. | Facts, definitions, actor/action relationships, exact term, and artifact identity. | Explain faithfully in familiar language, then preserve the exact technical term and source link. | Adding a causal claim or definition, dumping jargon, or simplifying away technical meaning. |
| `IMPACT` | **[D]** Williams' cost/consequence, Minto's effects, and *Busy Readers*' relevance operation. | Recorded consequence, affected entity, severity or blocking state, uncertainty, and caveat. | Compress and position the supplied consequence without re-grading it; keep its evidence link visible. | Inferring significance or severity, deleting a caveat, or strengthening the source claim. |
| `ACTION` | **[D]** *Busy Readers* supports a bounded ask; Minto distinguishes next steps from disputed recommendations. | Exact required action or decision identity, options and consequences, and any producer-authored recommendation. `No action required` is valid only when the producer records that condition. | Put the complete ask together and make it prominent. Preserve consequential option labels and meanings; distinguish a Report-owned route suggestion from a producer-owned choice recommendation. | Inventing an action, option, preferred choice, or “no action” conclusion; treating navigation or continuation as approval. |

### 5.2 Overall assessment

- **[D]** The block has unusually good coverage of the questions a busy technical reviewer
  asks: What is this? Why am I seeing it? What follows from it? What must I do?
- **[D]** Its repeated order can make several topics easier to compare and scan.
- **[S]** It should be treated as a candidate grammar for **multi-topic decision/review
  reports**, provided every substantive field comes from the producer. An absent action is a
  named gap unless the producer explicitly records `No action required`.
- **[S]** It should not automatically replace formats for a single answer, raw evidence audit,
  procedure, or chronological incident narrative. Those jobs may need a different grammar.
- **[S]** The strongest unresolved ordering question is whether `ACTION` remains last for
  informed review or moves earlier for producer-marked urgent/blocking decisions. The books
  support point-first and necessary-context-first arrangements under different conditions;
  an Astra evaluation should compare decision accuracy, time, and authority preservation,
  not preference alone.

### 5.3 Review status and response recording

Redish supports a durable review object, section-specific comments, deliberate disposition,
and a summary of changes made and not made. The proposed symbols—`UR`, `RV`, `CM`, `RJ`,
`AP`, `DF`—go beyond the source and mix four independently owned dimensions:

| Dimension | Owner and record | What it may establish | What it may not establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Delivery / revelation** | Report records a receipt-confirmed preview or section body against its stable ID. | What Report actually presented and when. | That the user read, understood, reviewed, or accepted it. |
| **Review / comment** | The user explicitly marks a section under review, reviewed, or deferred and authors comments; Report stores the exact statement or immutable reference. | What the user said about their review and the presence of their comment. | A decision the user did not state or a producer's disposition of the comment. |
| **User outcome** | The user supplies an approval, rejection, requested change, or deferral in response to a producer-owned decision. Report may mirror the exact response as non-authoritative session history. | The user's expressed outcome, without paraphrase or strengthening. | Authoritative lifecycle recording or a transition on its own. |
| **Producer lifecycle disposition** | The producer records the user's outcome and accepts, rejects, investigates, or applies comments in its authoritative artifact. | The lifecycle consequence and made/not-made disposition. | A rewriting of the user's original response. |

A section can have a delivered body, an explicit `RV` response, two comments, and a pending
producer disposition at the same time. One symbol would discard at least one fact. `AP` and
`RJ` are safe only when visibly qualified as an exact **user response**; the producer's
artifact is the sole authority for lifecycle disposition.

- **Original advice [D]:** review a stable draft and preserve comment disposition across
  rounds. **Astra integration [A]:** use the internal Report artifact as the shared section
  index, local revelation record, and exact-response carrier.
- **[S]** Keep the four axes separate. Report may append delivery facts and immutable user
  responses, but must never autonomously edit a response, infer review from delivery, turn
  `RV` into `AP`, or apply the requested change.
- **[S]** If one visible symbol is desired, it must name one axis and owner, for example
  `[R:detail delivered]` or `[U:reviewed]`; comment count and producer disposition remain
  separate.

Whether compact codes or plain words are preferable remains a product decision; none of the
books validates the proposed vocabulary.

## 6. Three candidate report structures

These are comparison candidates derived from the books. They do not endorse the current
Capsule/NOW/NEXT/menu design, and none is approved by this research artifact. To prevent a
format example from masquerading as a Report-authored case, the samples use placeholders and
this non-normative provenance key:

- `{P}` = semantic content supplied by the producer;
- `{R}` = wording, position, navigation, or revelation fact supplied by Report;
- `{U}` = an exact user-authored response, shown only after one exists;
- `{LP}` = authoritative lifecycle disposition recorded by the producer.

The braces explain ownership in these samples; they are not a proposed user-interface syntax.

### Candidate A — Repeated five-field topic sections

Best fit: deliberate review of several producer-defined findings or deliverables with
complete case fields, where the reader needs the same orientation for each.

```text
{R: position} 2/5  {P: topic T-02} <producer-supplied topic label>
  [MOTIVATION | P:T-02.context] <producer-supplied purpose or relevance>
  [WHAT IS IT? | P:T-02.claim; R:plain wording] <faithful explanation>
  [IMPACT | P:T-02.consequence] <producer-supplied consequence and caveat>
  [ACTION | P:D-04] <producer-supplied action or complete decision; no invented option>
  [SOURCE | R] <links to the producer references used above>
```

- **Original advice [D]:** the sequence provides topic, motivation, consequence, and response
  structure. **Astra integration [S]:** producer references populate every substantive field;
  Report supplies the repeated route and stable source links; the user supplies any response.
- **[S]** A separate local record may say `{R: body delivered}` or quote `{U: exact comment}`.
  Only a producer artifact may add `{LP: disposition}`.
- **Risk:** repeating five labels can become verbose when a field adds no information; urgent
  action appears only after four lines.

### Candidate B — Consequence/action-first topic section

Best fit: a producer has explicitly marked an approval request, blocker, incident, or other
case where its recorded consequence warrants putting the ask first.

```text
{R: position} 2/5  {P: topic T-02 and action-required flag}
  [ACTION | P:D-04, unchanged semantics] <producer decision and all supplied options>
  [IMPACT | P:D-04.consequences] <producer consequence and qualification>
  [WHAT IS IT? | P:T-02.claim; R:plain wording] <faithful explanation>
  [MOTIVATION | P:T-02.context] <producer-supplied reason this decision is live>
  [SOURCE | R] <links to D-04, T-02, and evidence>
```

- **Original advice [D]:** this is consistent with governing-point-first and low-response-
  effort guidance. **Astra integration [S]:** Report reorders a complete producer-owned
  decision envelope; it does not author its urgency, options, consequences, or preference.
- **Risk:** the reader may act before understanding context if options and consequences are
  compressed too aggressively.

### Candidate C — Textual outline followed by one selected topic

Best fit: progressive review of a long artifact where the user first needs a map, not a panel
of conversational “choices.”

```text
REPORT OUTLINE
{R} 1/5  {P:T-01} <topic label> — {R:faithful preview of P conclusion and caveat}
{R} 2/5  {P:T-02} <topic label> — {R:faithful preview of P conclusion and caveat}
{R} 3/5  {P:T-03} <topic label> — {R:faithful preview of P conclusion and caveat}
{R} 4/5  {P:T-04} <topic label> — {U:comment exists, only if explicitly recorded}
{R} 5/5  {P:T-05} <topic label> — {R:detail not yet delivered}

CURRENT SECTION
{R} 2/5  {P:T-02} <topic label>
  [MOTIVATION | P] ...
  [WHAT IS IT? | P facts; R wording] ...
  [IMPACT | P] ...
  [ACTION | P] ...
```

- **Original advice [D]:** Redish supports a descriptive heading outline, and Minto supports
  discussion one portion at a time. **Astra integration [S]:** the producer supplies each
  topic and preview semantics; Report constructs the outline and faithful previews.
- **Original advice [A]:** segmenting and learner-control research supports visible routes and
  bounded pacing while warning that navigation costs effort. **Astra integration [S]:** Report
  selects one producer-defined section for revelation; the user may select another or respond.
- **[S]** The outline and internal Report artifact can share section IDs. Revealing a section
  updates its local `{R}` delivery record; it does not imply `{U}` review or `{LP}` lifecycle
  disposition and does not require a project-wide exposure file.
- **Risk:** progressive delivery can hide cross-topic dependencies or force excessive
  navigation. Every producer-identified required action, dependency, and material caveat must
  remain visible enough that Report's route cannot misstate the case.

## 7. Design and evaluation consequences—not decisions

The books support investigating these questions, not assuming their answers:

1. Does the repeated five-field block improve decision accuracy and issue prioritization over
   a flat technical report containing the same evidence?
2. For blocking topics, does action-first improve time to correct action without reducing
   comprehension of consequence and caveat?
3. Does a textual outline plus one-section-at-a-time review lower burden, or do selection and
   return costs offset the benefit?
4. Can readers correctly distinguish review progress, comment presence, and authoritative
   lifecycle disposition when delivery, review/comment, user outcome, and producer disposition
   are rendered separately?
5. Does “plain description first, exact term second” reduce terminology confusion without
   weakening technical precision?
6. How often does a rendered brief introduce an **unauthorized substantive inference**—a
   purpose, relevance judgment, conclusion, consequence, action, or essentialness claim that
   has no producer-owned source field?
7. How often does Report create an **invented option or preference**, or let a route suggestion
   appear to recommend a substantive user choice?

Primary outcomes should include correct decisions, severe items missed, time to identify the
required action, evidence inspection, resumption/navigation errors, unauthorized-inference
rate, and invented-option/preference rate. Preference and perceived workload are useful
secondary outcomes, not substitutes for correctness or authority preservation.

### 7.1 Design disposition recorded 2026-08-19

The user approved the five-field route as a **conditional semantic skeleton**, not a mandatory
literal template. Astra Report will normally use `TOPIC / MOTIVATION / WHAT IS IT? / IMPACT /
ACTION` for several comparable explanatory or decision topics, while merging redundant visible
fields, omitting explicitly inapplicable ones, and moving `ACTION` first for a producer-marked
blocker or live decision. Progress, failure, incident, evidence-audit, and single-answer reports
retain job-specific structures.

The user also rejected one-topic-at-a-time Standard delivery. Standard now means one consolidated
textual response containing the producer-supported governing outcome and all material top-level
topics; evidence and audit depth remain addressable without a required conversational turn per
topic. Decision panels are reserved for actual producer decisions and final confirmation of
polished user feedback. This is a product decision informed by the source synthesis, not a claim
that the books validated Astra's exact format.

- *Writing for Busy Readers* is an ebook-derived PDF without stable printed pagination; all

- The Redish and Williams EPUBs contain printed-page markers; extraction can split paragraphs

- The Handbook and *e-Learning* PDFs use two-column layouts in places. Chapter headings,

- The instructional works study learning, often with multimedia and novice learners. Their
  implications for text-based technical supervision are labeled analogical.
- The collection does not empirically establish an optimal number of topics, a five-field

  runtime behavior.
- This is not a full-book synopsis. Omitting an irrelevant chapter means it was outside the
  reporting question, not that the chapter lacks value.

## 9. Authority boundary

Nothing in these books gives Astra Report the conventional author's full authority. The
binding division for every finding in this distillation is:

- **Producer owns the case:** truth claims, communicative purpose, relevance, consequence,
  severity, blockers, requirements, source relationships, essentialness, action, decision
  options, substantive recommendation, work state, and lifecycle disposition.
- **Report owns the route through it:** faithful wording, ordering, headings, bounded grouping
  of already-related material, pacing, section markers, navigation, revelation, and evidence
  links. A route recommendation may say which section to inspect next; it cannot say which
  substantive option to choose unless the producer supplied that recommendation.
- **User owns the outcome:** review declarations, comments, approvals, rejections, requested
  changes, and deferrals. Report may preserve the user's exact response, but does not infer
  comprehension or strengthen, edit, or interpret that response.
- **Producer records lifecycle effect:** the producer's authoritative artifact alone records
  how the user's outcome affects approval, comment disposition, work, or progression.

The internal Report artifact is therefore a valid de-facto **local delivery/revelation and
response record**, not a new source of engineering or lifecycle truth. It may record which
section was receipt-confirmed as shown and append an exact user response or immutable
reference. It may not infer review from delivery, turn `reviewed` into `approved`, invent a
missing field, apply a comment, resolve a source conflict, or start the next workflow. When a
producer input is missing, Report names the gap or shows exact sourced material.

The design question opened by this distillation is therefore narrow:

> Can the internal Report artifact provide a clearer route through a producer-owned case and
> preserve local revelation and exact user responses—using a repeated plain-language section
> grammar where appropriate—without claiming the producer's case or the user's outcome?

That question should be discussed against the candidate structures above before any existing
design or plan is amended.
